Body Composition 2026

Waist-to-height ratio check

Since January 1, 2026 the Marine Corps screens body composition by waist-to-height ratio instead of height and weight: 0.52 or less, the same for men and women (MARADMIN 066/26, as changed by 073/26). Enter your height and your waist measured at the navel.

Your maximum waist for your height appears here.

Both measurements are rounded down to the nearest 1/2 inch, as the MARADMIN directs, and checked against its height and maximum waist table. Over the limit means a body fat evaluation (tape test or bioelectrical impedance) against the MCO 6110.3A body fat standards. Marines with 250+ on both the PFT and CFT get an extra 1%; 285+ on both allows up to 26% (male) or 36% (female). Unofficial tool: your unit's measurement is the record.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the Marine PFT?

The Marine Corps Physical Fitness Test (PFT) measures physical readiness with three events: pull-ups or push-ups, the forearm plank (crunches were discontinued at the end of 2022), and a 3-mile run. Each is worth up to 100 points, for a maximum of 300. It is required for all active duty and reserve Marines.

What is a passing PFT score?

You need at least 150 of 300 points and the minimum on every event. Classes are the same for every age and sex under MCO 6100.13A Table 2-1: 1st Class 235 to 300, 2nd Class 200 to 234, and 3rd Class 150 to 199. Missing any event minimum fails the PFT whatever the total.

How many pull-ups do you need for the Marine PFT?

For 100 points, men need 18 to 23 pull-ups depending on age group (23 from age 21 to 35) and women need 4 to 12. The event minimum is 3 to 5 pull-ups for men and 1 to 4 for women. Every age group is on the score chart.

Can you do push-ups instead of pull-ups on the PFT?

Yes. Marines choose pull-ups or push-ups for the upper body event, but push-ups top out at 70 points while pull-ups are worth up to 100. Men need 64 to 87 push-ups for the 70-point max depending on age group, and women need 38 to 50. The calculator scores both.

What is the plank standard for the Marine PFT?

The plank table is the same for every Marine: a 3:45 hold earns 100 points and 1:10 is the 40-point minimum.

What is the 3-mile run standard for the Marine PFT?

100 points is 18:00 for men and 21:00 for women through age 40, easing to 19:30 and 22:30 at 51 and over. The slowest passing time is 27:40 for men aged 17 to 25 and 30:50 for women aged 17 to 25.

What changed for the Marine PFT in 2026?

Two things, both from 1 January 2026. Marines with a combat arms PMOS are scored on the male PFT tables and must reach 210 (MARADMIN 613/25). And body composition is screened by waist-to-height ratio, 0.52 or less, instead of height and weight (MARADMIN 066/26 and 073/26).

What is the combat arms PFT standard for 2026?

Under MARADMIN 613/25, effective January 1, 2026, Marines with a combat arms PMOS (infantry, artillery, assault amphibian, combat engineer, and EOD specialties) are scored on the male, age-normed PFT tables and must reach at least 210 points. A combat arms Marine who passes but scores under 210 is placed on remedial physical training and may face PMOS reclassification or promotion restriction.

What is the Marine Corps waist-to-height ratio standard?

Since January 1, 2026, the Marine Corps screens body composition with a waist-to-height ratio of 0.52 or less for men and women (MARADMIN 066/26 and 073/26), replacing the height and weight check. Waist is measured at the navel and both measurements are rounded down to the nearest half inch. Marines over the limit get a body fat evaluation by tape test or bioelectrical impedance.

When do Marines take the PFT?

Active duty Marines take the PFT once each calendar year between 1 January and 30 June under MCO 6100.13A, and the CFT between 1 July and 31 December. Score your CFT on the Marine CFT calculator.

What order governs the Marine PFT?

Marine Corps Order MCO 6100.13A, with Admin Change 5, sets the PFT and CFT events, scoring tables, classes, and testing calendar.

Is this an official USMC PFT calculator?

No. It is an independent tool scored on the official MCO 6100.13A tables, not affiliated with or endorsed by the U.S. Marine Corps or Department of Defense. The score of record is the one your unit enters in MCTIMS.
