How to read the Air Force PFA score charts

DAFMAN 36-2905 has a separate chart for each exercise, by sex and five-year age group. Each chart turns a result — reps, seconds, laps, or a waist-to-height ratio — into points. Add the four components and you have your composite out of 100.

Ratings

What the composite means.

Excellent

90+

Composite of 90.0 or higher, with every component above zero.

Satisfactory

75–89.9

The passing band, again with every component above zero.

Unsatisfactory

<75

A composite under 75, or a zero in any single component, whatever the total.

Reading a chart

From a result to a composite.

  • Find the chart for your exercise, then the column for your sex and age group (under 25, 25–29, and so on up to 60+).
  • Find your result. You score the points for the line you actually reached: a time has to be at or under the line, reps or laps at or over it.
  • Do the same for each component: cardio (up to 50), body composition (20), strength (15) and core (15).
  • Add them up. Then check that no component scored zero; one zero makes the whole assessment Unsatisfactory.

This page doesn’t reproduce the charts. Point values depend on age, sex and exercise; the manual and your unit fitness program manager are the official source.

What moves the score

The short version.

Cardio carries the most

A scoring run or HAMR is worth 35 to 50 points, more than strength and core together.

Body composition is a fifth

Waist-to-height is worth 20 points: all of them at 0.49 or less, none at 0.60 or more.

Strength and core decide close calls

Each runs from 2.5 to 15 points, which is often the difference near 75 or 90.
