Altitude Adjustment

Air Force altitude adjustment matters if you are testing above 5,000 feet.

Most people do not need to think about altitude adjustment until they actually do. If your test location is high enough, it can affect how the aerobic event should be handled under current standards, which is why the calculator includes altitude adjustment support.

Quick Take

Why it matters.

Different test environment

Running at elevation is not the same as running at lower altitude, and people want to know whether that changes scoring.

Closer pass/fail margins

If you are near a score cutoff, even a small change in outcome can feel important.

Cleaner planning

It is easier to prep well when you know whether altitude needs to be part of the conversation before test day.

What To Remember

Altitude is not relevant for every test, but it matters where it applies.

  • The key threshold most people care about is testing above 5,000 feet.
  • If that applies to your location, you do not want to ignore it when checking the aerobic score.
  • The simplest approach is using a calculator that already includes altitude adjustment support rather than trying to sort it out manually.
Practical Use

It is really about avoiding bad assumptions.

If your test is at altitude, it makes sense to check the score in the same conditions you expect to test under. That is easier than finding out late that you were planning against the wrong result.
