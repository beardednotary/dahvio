Hazmat Reference

DOT hazmat placards, explained

Every hazmat placard on the road belongs to one of nine DOT hazard classes defined under 49 CFR — identified by class number, color, and symbol. This page is a plain-language reference to the classes, how UN numbers and ERG guide numbers fit in, and how DOT placards differ from the GHS pictograms you'll see on a shipping label.

UN Numbers

The four-digit code on the shipping paper

A UN number identifies the specific material inside — not just its hazard class. Two shipments can carry the same placard but different UN numbers, and the UN number is what a first responder or dispatcher actually looks up.

UN1075Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG)2.1
UN1203Gasoline3

Each UN number also maps to an ERG guide number — a two- or three-digit page reference in the Emergency Response Guidebook covering potential hazards, safety distances, and initial response steps for that material.

Common Confusion

DOT placard vs. GHS pictogram

DOT Placard

Required under 49 CFR on vehicles and bulk containers in transport. Identifies hazard class for responders, carriers, and inspectors on the road.

GHS Pictogram

Required under OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard on product labels and Safety Data Sheets. Warns workers handling the container at the workplace.

The same material can carry both at once — a transport placard on the tanker and a GHS pictogram on the drum inside it. They're answering different questions for different audiences, which is why HazMat Pro keeps a glossary for both alongside the placard and UN number data.

FAQ

Common questions

How many DOT hazmat placard classes are there?

Nine hazard classes, several split into numbered divisions — like 2.1 Flammable Gas or 4.3 Dangerous When Wet — for 19 placard divisions in total.

What is a UN number?

A four-digit code identifying the specific material being shipped, such as UN1203 for gasoline. It's what shipping papers and lookups actually key off of, not just the hazard class.

What is an ERG guide number?

A page reference in the Emergency Response Guidebook that a UN number maps to, covering hazards, safety distances, and initial response steps for that material.

What's the difference between a DOT placard and a GHS pictogram?

Placards mark transport vehicles under DOT rules; GHS pictograms mark product labels and SDS sheets under OSHA rules. The same material can carry both at the same time.
