SJT Prep Sheet

How to think through WAPS SJT questions

This is not about memorizing canned answers. Strong SJT performance usually comes from recognizing what the most professional, mission-supporting, and people-aware response looks like when the situation gets messy.

What the best answers usually do

  • Protect the mission without ignoring people.
  • Use the chain of command appropriately instead of bypassing it for drama.
  • Address problems early, directly, and professionally.
  • Show accountability and judgment instead of avoidance.

What the weakest answers usually look like

  • They are passive when action is clearly needed.
  • They escalate too hard or too early without trying reasonable first steps.
  • They protect comfort or popularity over standards.
  • They create a second problem while trying to fix the first one.

Quick decision filter

  • Does this response support standards, fairness, and professionalism?
  • Does it solve the actual problem instead of avoiding discomfort?
  • Would a strong NCO or supervisor defend this choice afterward?
  • Does it create unnecessary conflict, embarrassment, or risk?

Common trap to watch for

The most aggressive answer is not automatically the best answer.

Sometimes the right choice is calm correction, private counseling, or measured follow-up instead of the answer that feels the toughest. SJT often rewards judgment, not intensity.

How to study SJT better

  • Review why each answer was good or bad, not just whether you got it right.
  • Track misses by pattern: avoidance, overreaction, poor prioritization, or leadership tone.
  • Practice in short blocks so the reasoning stays sharp.
  • Revisit flagged scenarios after a day or two and see whether your reasoning improves.
