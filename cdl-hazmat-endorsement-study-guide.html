CDL Hazmat Endorsement

Studying for the CDL hazmat endorsement

The hazmat ("H") endorsement lets a CDL holder legally transport placarded quantities of hazardous materials. Getting it takes a written knowledge test plus a TSA background check. This page covers what the test actually asks, the facts worth knowing cold, and a set of practice questions to check yourself against.

What's Tested

The knowledge test topics

Why the regs exist

The purpose of the Hazardous Materials Regulations and the roles of shippers, carriers, and drivers.

Communication rules

Shipping papers, package marking, labeling, and placarding — how a load is identified from the paperwork to the outside of the vehicle.

Loading & unloading

Segregation rules, blocking and bracing, and materials that can't be loaded together.

Bulk packaging

Marking, loading, and unloading rules specific to cargo tanks and other bulk containers.

Security awareness

Recognizing security risks, controlling access to your load, and following your carrier's security plan.

Emergency response

What documentation must be in the vehicle and what to do — and not do — if there's an incident.

Know Cold

Facts that show up on the test

  • Placarding thresholds — Table 1 materials (like Division 1.1–1.3 explosives, Division 2.3 poison gas, and Packing Group I poison-inhalation-hazard materials) require placards at any quantity. Table 2 materials require placards once the aggregate gross weight reaches 1,001 lbs (454 kg).
  • Shipping papers — must be within immediate reach while driving, easily seen by anyone entering the cab, and — if you leave the vehicle — left on the driver's seat or in the driver's door pouch.
  • No-smoking rule — no smoking within 25 feet of a placarded cargo tank carrying Class 3 or Division 2.1 materials, or a placarded cargo compartment carrying Division 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 4.2, 4.3, or Class 5 materials.
  • Segregation — some hazard classes can't be loaded, transported, or stored together and must be separated per the segregation table (for example, certain explosives can't ride with certain oxidizers or flammables).
  • Security plans — carriers required to placard must maintain a written security plan addressing personnel security, unauthorized access, and en route security.
  • Leaking packages — a driver must not transport a package that is known to be leaking or damaged; it has to be isolated and reported per procedure, not driven on.

State CDL manuals vary slightly in wording, but these core facts are consistent nationally because they come from the federal Hazardous Materials Regulations (49 CFR Parts 171–180).

Practice Questions

Check yourself

Tap a question to reveal the answer and a short explanation.

1. A placarded cargo tank is carrying a Division 2.1 (flammable gas) load. How close can someone smoke to it?

Not within 25 feet. The 25-foot no-smoking rule applies to placarded cargo tanks used for Class 3 and Division 2.1 materials, and to placarded cargo compartments carrying Divisions 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 4.2, 4.3, or Class 5 materials.

2. Where must hazmat shipping papers be kept while you're driving?

Within immediate reach, clearly visible to anyone entering the cab. If you leave the vehicle, they must be left on the driver's seat or in a pouch on the driver's door — never locked away out of sight.

3. What separates a "Table 1" hazmat from a "Table 2" hazmat for placarding purposes?

Quantity thresholds. Table 1 materials require placards regardless of amount. Table 2 materials only require placards once the aggregate gross weight of all Table 2 hazmat on the vehicle reaches 1,001 lbs (454 kg).

4. You discover a hazmat package is leaking during a pre-trip inspection. What should you do?

Do not transport it. A driver may not transport a package known to be leaking, damaged, or otherwise non-compliant. Isolate it and report it per your carrier's procedure instead of continuing the trip.

5. What does a carrier's written security plan primarily address?

Personnel security, unauthorized access, and en route security — required for carriers transporting hazmat quantities that require placarding, so a load isn't left accessible to anyone who shouldn't have it.

6. Can all hazard classes be loaded onto the same vehicle together?

No. Certain hazard class combinations are prohibited or restricted under the segregation rules — for example, some explosives can't be loaded with certain oxidizers or flammable materials. Always check the segregation table before mixed loads.

7. Besides the written knowledge test, what else is required to get a hazmat endorsement?

A TSA Security Threat Assessment — a background check that includes fingerprinting. Both the test and the background check must be completed and passed.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the CDL hazmat endorsement?

The "H" endorsement on a CDL, required to legally transport placarded quantities of hazardous materials. It requires a written knowledge test and a TSA background check.

What topics are on the test?

Why the regulations exist, shipping papers and placarding, loading/unloading and segregation, bulk packaging, security awareness, and emergency response information.

Does it require a background check?

Yes — a TSA Security Threat Assessment, including fingerprinting, in addition to the knowledge test.

Is HazMat Pro official CDL test prep?
