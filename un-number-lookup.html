Why It Matters

One code, three lookups

A UN number identifies the specific material inside a shipment — not just its hazard class. Two loads can carry the same placard but different UN numbers, and the UN number is what a dispatcher, driver, or first responder actually keys off of when pulling shipping papers or calling in an incident. That one four-digit code resolves to a hazard class, a packing group, and an ERG guide number for response guidance — this tool does all three lookups at once.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a UN number?

A four-digit code identifying the specific material being shipped, such as UN1203 for gasoline or UN1830 for sulfuric acid. It's listed on shipping papers and often on the placard or an orange panel.

How do I find the ERG guide number for a UN number?

Search above by UN number or material name. Each result includes the ERG guide number — the response guide page in the Emergency Response Guidebook for that material.

What does the packing group column mean?

PG I is greatest danger, PG II is medium, PG III is minor. Gases and some other classes don't use a packing group.
