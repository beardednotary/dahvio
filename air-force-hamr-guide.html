The HAMR: the PFA’s shuttle-run option

The 20-meter High Aerobic Multi-shuttle Run (HAMR) is one of the two cardio events in the Air Force PFA; the other is the 2-mile run. It is worth the same 50 points, and it rewards rhythm as much as fitness.

How it works

Back and forth, on the beep.

  • You run 20 meters between two lines, arriving at each line on an audio cue.
  • The cues come closer together as the levels go up, so every lap is a little faster than the last.
  • The test ends when you can no longer keep pace; the manual sets exactly how missed cues are counted.
  • Your result is the number of laps you completed.
Scoring

Laps, read against a chart.

Like the run, the HAMR has its own chart for each sex and age group. A scoring result earns between 35 and 50 points; fewer laps than the minimum scores zero, which fails the assessment. On the chart for men under 25, for example, 87 laps earns the full 50 and 42 laps is the lowest scoring result, worth 35.

Run or HAMR

Choosing your cardio event.

Why people choose it

Some people do better with a building stop-and-go pace than with holding a steady pace for two miles.

What makes it tricky

Turns cost time and the pace never lets up. Early laps run too fast are paid for later.

How to practice

Practice to the real cue cadence over the full 20 meters, so the turns and the timing are familiar before test day. The Air Force PT Calculator iOS app includes a HAMR timer that plays the cues over your music.

To see what a lap count is worth alongside your other components, use the Air Force PT calculator.
